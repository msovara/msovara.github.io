01 / About

Ocean, atmosphere, and the machines in between

I am a research software engineer and climate scientist based in South Africa. By day I work at the Centre for High Performance Computing, part of the CSIR, where I help scientists run weather, climate, ocean, and space-science codes on the national Lengau cluster. The rest of the time I am a doctoral candidate at the University of Cape Town, in ocean and climate dynamics.

The thread is the same in both roles. Southern Africa’s rainfall is tightly coupled to the Indian Ocean. Useful prediction therefore needs three things at once: physical understanding, models that actually compile and scale, and software that other people can reuse. I work at that intersection.

I hold an MSc in Ocean and Climate Dynamics from UCT and a BSc Honours in Environmental Sciences from Walter Sisulu University in Mthatha. Before CHPC I interned in marine remote sensing at Oceans and Coasts. I also founded MBV Climate and Ocean Intelligence Africa, a small practice for climate and ocean decision support on the continent.

02 / Research

What I am trying to understand

Teleconnections

Indian Ocean Dipole and African drought

I analyse how IOD events, with and without ENSO, organise drought across Africa from 1980 to 2020. The work uses ERA5, SPI and SPEI, lag and partial correlations, wavelet coherence, and circulation composites: MSLP, 850 hPa winds, and 500 hPa height. The practical question is lead time. If the Indian Ocean is already rearranging the atmosphere, how much warning does southern Africa actually have?

Dynamical models

Can ICON and MPAS see the same teleconnection?

Statistical maps are only the first act. The next is whether non-hydrostatic global models, run on national HPC, reproduce the observed drought dipole, lag, and circulation. I deploy ICON, MPAS, WRF, CCAM, and CROCO on Lengau, then treat Paper 1 diagnostics as a benchmark rather than a one-off analysis.

AI weather

Forecasts that do not require a supercomputer in the room

Through ECMWF’s AI Weather Quest and Code for Earth, I work on Anemoi pipelines, JRA-3Q data preparation, and Mvula (LapAI-Forecast): compressing AIFS-class models so a 10-day global forecast can be evaluated for African conditions on modest hardware. Edge-deployable climate intelligence matters where connectivity and compute are uneven.

03 / Software

Codes that survive contact with Lengau

05 / Teaching

Capacity is also software

A large part of CHPC work is teaching by other means: debugging someone else’s namelist at 07:00, writing the guide that prevents the next ten tickets, and showing postgraduate students from across southern Africa how to move from a laptop script to a cluster job.

I contribute to the CHPC Winter School, the DSI Coding School, and a coding and robotics curriculum designed with the Department of Basic Education. I have been involved in the CHPC Student Cluster Competition, SASAS, the CHPC–ASSAf collaboration, and the RSSE Africa community. The audience ranges from high-school teachers to working scientists. The point is the same: computation should not be a private language.
